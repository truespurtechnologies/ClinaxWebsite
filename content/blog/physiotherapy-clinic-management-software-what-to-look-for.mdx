Most physiotherapy clinics in India go looking for software at the same moment: the spreadsheet has a second tab, the WhatsApp group has a second admin, and somebody missed a follow-up that mattered. The market answers with a long list of products that all describe themselves the same way. This checklist is meant to help you tell them apart — not by feature count, but by whether the product is built for how a physiotherapy clinic actually runs.

## Start from the session, not the appointment

A GP visit is short and ends in a prescription. A physiotherapy visit is a session: it begins by reviewing what happened last time, moves through assessment and treatment, produces notes that other therapists will depend on, updates goals, and ends with a home exercise plan the patient has to follow until the next visit.

Software designed for general clinics models a visit as a billing event with a notes box attached. That is why physiotherapy clinics using generic software usually keep the clinical story somewhere else — a paper file, a therapist's memory, a chat thread.

**What to look for:** does the product have a structured session record? Can you see assessment, treatment log, goals, progress and home exercises as distinct, carried-forward parts of the patient's history, or is it one free-text field per visit?

## Ask who the software is for

Three groups of people use clinic software every day, and they need different things:

- **Reception** needs today's arrivals, the waiting queue, check-in, bookings and a follow-up list that does not disappear when the whiteboard is wiped.
- **Therapists** need the patient's history at the point of care — on the treatment floor, not only at a desk — plus today's priorities and whatever documentation is still pending.
- **Owners and managers** need to know what is happening across the clinic: appointments, completions, collections, therapist utilisation and follow-ups, per branch and overall.

Many products are built for only one of these groups and bolt the others on. The tell is a single generic screen everyone has to adapt to.

**What to look for:** role-based views. Each role should open the same record and see the layout suited to its work.

## Plan for the second branch before you need it

Single-branch clinics often assume multi-branch features are for someone else. In practice the second branch is the moment the current tools stop working, and the migration is far harder once two locations are running on spreadsheets.

**What to look for:** branch-aware scheduling, the ability to decide who sees which branches and records, and dashboards that can be read per branch or for the whole clinic — available on the same foundation you start with, not a different product tier that requires starting over.

## Check what happens to your data

Patient information is sensitive, and clinic owners in India are increasingly aware of the Digital Personal Data Protection Act. You do not need to be a lawyer to ask the right questions.

**What to look for:**

- Encryption in transit and at rest.
- Role-based access, so staff see only what their work requires.
- Automatic backups.
- Full export at any time, in a usable format, with no lock-in.

Be wary of certification claims you cannot verify. A vendor that explains plainly how data is protected and lets you export it is usually a safer bet than one that leads with badges.

## Understand how onboarding actually works

The best software fails if the clinic never finishes moving onto it. Ask how the vendor runs onboarding:

- Do they start with priority workflows — typically front desk and scheduling — rather than switching everything at once?
- Will they import your existing patient list from spreadsheets or exports, so reception is not re-typing records on day one?
- Is each role onboarded separately, with support through the first weeks?
- When you add therapists, specialties or a branch later, is it configuration on the same system or a new project?

## Look at how pricing is structured

Per-user pricing, per-branch pricing, flat tiers and scoped pricing all exist. None is wrong, but the structure should match how your clinic will grow. A plan that is cheap for one branch and three therapists may be very different at two branches and eight. Ask for the price of the clinic you expect to be in two years, not only the one you are today.

## A short checklist

| Question | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Is the session record structured (assessment, treatment, goals, progress, home exercises)? | This is the clinical core of physiotherapy; a notes box is not enough. |
| Does each role get its own working view? | Reception, therapists and owners need different things from the same record. |
| Is it branch-aware from the start? | The second branch is where fragmented tools break. |
| Can we export everything, any time? | Your data should stay yours. |
| How does onboarding proceed, and what is imported? | Adoption, not features, decides whether the software works. |
| What does it cost at the size we expect to be? | Pricing structure should fit your growth path. |
