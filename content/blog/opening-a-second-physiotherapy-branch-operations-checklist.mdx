The second branch is usually a sign of success: demand has outgrown one location, a trusted therapist is ready to lead a team, a neighbourhood is under-served. It is also the point where the operational habits of a single clinic stop working — and most of the pain is not clinical, it is coordination.

This checklist is about the operational side of opening a second physiotherapy branch: what changes, what to decide before day one, and what to put in place so the owner is not driving between locations to find out what is happening.

## What actually changes

With one branch the owner is in the building. Problems are visible because they are in the room; questions are answered by walking to reception. With two branches:

- You cannot see both floors.
- Patients move between locations and expect their history to come with them.
- Scheduling has to account for therapist availability at two sites.
- Reception at each site needs its own view, but you need the whole picture.
- Follow-ups, collections and utilisation have to be compared across branches, not just totalled.

The tools that worked for one site — the diary, the group chat, the spreadsheet — cannot answer a simple question across two.

## Before day one: decisions

### 1. One patient record, or two?

Decide explicitly that a patient is one record regardless of where they are treated. This sounds obvious; in practice, clinics with separate spreadsheets per branch end up with duplicate patients within weeks. Whatever system you use must hold one record per patient with visibility across branches, subject to the access you configure.

### 2. Who sees what

Write down the roles before you open: branch reception, branch therapists, branch manager (if any), and owner. Then decide which branches and which records each role can see. A branch manager might see only their branch; the owner sees every branch and the overall picture. Doing this on paper first makes it easy to configure, and hard to forget.

### 3. How scheduling works across sites

Will therapists work at both branches? If so, availability must be visible per branch and in one combined view, so that reception at either site can see where capacity is before booking. Decide how cross-branch bookings and reschedules are handled.

### 4. Who owns follow-ups

At a single branch, follow-ups are often owned informally by whoever remembers. Across two branches, that stops working. Decide whether follow-ups are owned per branch or centrally, and make sure the list is produced from the record — not from memory.

### 5. What the owner needs to see weekly

List the handful of numbers you actually want to compare across branches: appointments, completions, collections, therapist utilisation, follow-ups, pending documentation. If the system cannot show these per branch and overall without someone building a spreadsheet, you will see them monthly at best.

## Setting up the new branch

- Configure the branch — its therapists, availability and reception view — before staff start.
- Import any existing patients the new location inherits, so reception is not re-typing on day one.
- Onboard by role — reception, therapists, manager — rather than in one all-hands session. Each role needs a short, specific walkthrough of their own view.
- Run the first weeks with support available: the questions that come up in week two are different from the ones in week one.

## Documentation consistency across teams

A second branch means therapists who may never have worked together are documenting the same kinds of sessions. A structured session workflow — review, assess, treat, document, plan, complete — keeps notes consistent so a patient seen at either site has a history any therapist can rely on. This is also what makes clinical reviews visible to leadership across both locations.

## Common mistakes

- **Copying the branch-one spreadsheet.** Two spreadsheets are not a multi-branch system; they are two single-branch systems with a reconciliation problem.
- **Postponing roles and access.** Access set up loosely at launch is rarely tightened later.
- **Treating reporting as a later problem.** The owner needs cross-branch visibility from the first week, when habits are forming.
- **Choosing a tool that needs re-platforming at branch three.** Whatever you adopt should add a branch as configuration, not as a new project.

## A compact checklist

- One patient record across branches, with configurable access.
- Roles written down: who sees which branches and records.
- Branch-aware scheduling with a combined availability view.
- Follow-up ownership decided; list produced from the record.
- Weekly numbers defined and visible per branch and overall.
- New branch configured, patients imported, staff onboarded by role.
- Structured documentation so notes are consistent across teams.
