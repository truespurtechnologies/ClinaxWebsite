Every physiotherapist learned SOAP — Subjective, Objective, Assessment, Plan — in training. Most clinics still struggle to get consistent notes out of it. The reason is rarely the therapists. It is that SOAP describes what a note should *contain*, not how the session should *flow*, and in a busy clinic the flow is what determines whether the note gets written well, late, or not at all.

This article is about turning SOAP from a template into a workflow that fits how a session actually runs, so documentation is consistent across every therapist and every visit.

## Why consistency matters more than completeness

A single very thorough note is less useful than a series of consistent ones. The value of documentation in physiotherapy is longitudinal: the next therapist needs to compare today with last time, see whether goals are moving, and know what the patient was asked to do at home. If one therapist writes two paragraphs and another writes two lines, comparison breaks down and the patient's story has gaps.

Consistency comes from structure that is the same every time, and from capturing information at the moment it is known rather than reconstructing it afterwards.

## The problem with the notes box

Many clinic systems implement SOAP as four free-text boxes, or as one notes field. Free text is flexible, which is exactly the problem: it lets each therapist invent their own structure, and it makes goals, progress and home exercises disappear into prose where nobody can carry them forward.

The alternative is to treat the session as a sequence of steps, each of which produces a distinct part of the record.

## A six-step session workflow

Here is one way to structure a session so that SOAP falls out of it naturally:

### 1. Review

Before anything else, the therapist sees the patient's history: previous assessments, the treatment log, current goals and the home exercise plan from last time. This takes a minute and changes the session. It also means the Subjective section is a conversation about what has changed, not a re-interview.

### 2. Assess

Subjective and objective findings are captured as the assessment happens. Keeping these separate — what the patient reports, what the therapist measures or observes — is the core of SOAP, and it is far easier to do during the assessment than to untangle afterwards.

### 3. Treat

The treatment delivered today is logged as a treatment log, not buried in a paragraph. A log can be compared across sessions; a paragraph cannot.

### 4. Document

The clinical reasoning — the Assessment in SOAP terms — is recorded with the findings and treatment already in place. Because the earlier steps captured the facts, this step is about judgement, and is correspondingly shorter.

### 5. Plan

Goals are updated, progress is noted against them, and the home exercise plan is set for the period until the next visit. These are kept as distinct, carried-forward elements so the next therapist starts from them rather than searching for them.

### 6. Complete

The visit is summarised and the next appointment and any follow-up are set. The follow-up now exists because the record produced it, not because someone remembered.

## What this gives the rest of the clinic

Structured documentation is not only for therapists.

- **Reception** gets a follow-up list produced from the clinical record.
- **Other therapists** get a history they can rely on, which matters most in multi-therapist and multi-branch clinics where a patient may not always see the same person.
- **Clinical leads and owners** can see pending documentation and clinical reviews awaiting a decision, because the record has structure that can be reported on.

## Practical tips for adoption

- **Document in the room.** The structure above works because each step captures information at the moment it is known. Notes written at the end of the day are reconstructed, and reconstruction is where inconsistency comes from.
- **Use the floor, not only the desk.** Whatever system you use should work where the treatment happens.
- **Agree the structure once.** Therapists should not have to decide per visit what goes where.
- **Review together occasionally.** A short monthly look at a few records across therapists catches drift early.

## Where this leads

Once sessions are documented in a consistent structure, two further things become possible. Progress against goals can be seen across visits rather than inferred.
